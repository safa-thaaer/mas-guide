ما هو مشروع OWASP MAS؟

قبل الدخول في التفاصيل، نرسم الخريطة: من يقف وراء المشروع، ومن أي أجزاء يتكوّن، وكيف ترتبط هذه الأجزاء ببعضها.

OWASP باختصار

OWASP (Open Worldwide Application Security Project) منظمة عالمية غير ربحية تنشر موارد مجانية ومفتوحة المصدر في أمن البرمجيات، ومن أشهرها قائمة OWASP Top 10. ومشروع MAS (Mobile Application Security) هو مشروعها الرائد (Flagship) المخصّص لتطبيقات الموبايل. كان اسمه سابقاً MSTG، ثم تغيّر إلى OWASP MAS في أغسطس 2022 ليعكس أنه أصبح إطار عمل كاملاً وليس مجرد دليل اختبار.

لماذا يحتاج الموبايل مشروعاً خاصاً؟

الهاتف ليس حاسوباً صغيراً، وتطبيق الموبايل ليس موقع ويب. أنظمة الموبايل تعزل التطبيقات عن بعضها، وطريقة توزيع التطبيقات مختلفة، وبعض الثغرات المعروفة في الويب تكون أقل أهمية هنا. في المقابل، الهاتف يحمل حياتنا كلها: الصور، الرسائل، الموقع، الحسابات البنكية، ويضيع أو يُسرق بسهولة.

الفكرة الأساسية

يقول تمهيد المعيار إن أمان الموبايل في جوهره هو حماية البيانات. نظاما Android وiOS يقدّمان أدوات قوية للتخزين الآمن والاتصال الآمن، لكنها لا تفيد إلا إذا استخدمها المطوّر بشكل صحيح.

What is OWASP MAS?

Before the details, let's draw the map: who is behind the project, which parts it is made of, and how those parts connect.

OWASP in a nutshell

OWASP (Open Worldwide Application Security Project) is a global non-profit that publishes free, open-source software security resources, the best known being the OWASP Top 10. MAS (Mobile Application Security) is its flagship project for mobile apps. It used to be called MSTG and was renamed OWASP MAS in August 2022, because it had grown into a complete framework rather than a single testing guide.

Why does mobile need its own project?

A phone is not a small computer, and a mobile app is not a website. Mobile operating systems isolate apps from each other, apps are distributed differently, and some well-known web vulnerability classes matter less here. On the other hand, the phone carries our whole life: photos, messages, location, bank accounts. And it is easily lost or stolen.

The key idea

The standard's foreword puts it simply: mobile security is, at its core, about data protection. Android and iOS provide mature APIs for secure storage and secure communication, but they only help when developers use them correctly.

The five parts of the project

MASVS

The standard

Answers: what must the app achieve? 24 security controls in 8 groups. For developers, architects and testers.

MASWE

The weakness list

Answers: which mistakes break those controls? 78 weaknesses, inspired by the global CWE list.

MASTG

The testing guide

Answers: how do I check the app in practice? Tests, techniques, tools and working examples for Android and iOS.

MAS Profiles

Testing profiles

Answers: how much protection does my app need? The L1, L2, R and P profiles.

MAS Crackmes

Hands-on challenges

"UnCrackable" apps for practicing reverse engineering, also used as examples throughout the MASTG.

The traceability chain: the idea that ties everything together

Every item in the project has a stable ID, and every item links to what sits above and below it. Here is a real example about random numbers:

  1. MASVS-CRYPTO-1 A general control: "The app employs current strong cryptography and uses it according to industry best practices." It names no platform and no specific mistake.
  2. MASWE-0012 A specific weakness: "Improper Random Number Generation". Still not tied to any platform.
  3. MASTG-TEST-0204 An Android test: "Insecure Random API Usage", with clear steps and a clear pass or fail condition.
  4. MASTG-DEMO-0007 A working example: Java code that uses Random() instead of SecureRandom(), plus the test script and the expected output.

Next to the test you will find MASTG-BEST-0001 (Use Secure Random Number Generator APIs), which tells the developer how to fix the problem, and MASTG-KNOW-0013, which explains the technical background on Android.

What about the MAS Checklist?

Earlier releases shipped an Excel sheet for tracking assessments. MASTG v2 no longer publishes it as an official file, because all the data (tests, weaknesses, controls and profiles) is now structured and cross-linked on the website and in the repositories, and any team can build the sheet it needs from it. The old files remain available for historical use.

المعيار MASVS

MASVS هو "دفتر الشروط" لأمان تطبيق الموبايل: قائمة قصيرة وواضحة من الضوابط يمكن قياس أي تطبيق عليها، مهما كانت طريقة بنائه.

ما هو MASVS؟

Mobile Application Security Verification Standard، أي معيار التحقق من أمان تطبيقات الموبايل. يُستخدم بثلاث طرق: مقياس نقيس به أمان التطبيق، ودليل يتبعه المطوّر أثناء البناء، وخط أساس يُشترط عند شراء تطبيق أو التعاقد على تطويره. الإصدار الحالي هو v2.1.0.

افتراضات المعيار

  • MASVS لا يغني عن ممارسات التطوير الآمن (Secure SDLC) مثل نمذجة التهديدات والمراجعة الأمنية، بل يكمّلها للجزء الخاص بالموبايل. ويشير إلى إطارات مثل OWASP SAMM وNIST SP 800-218 (SSDF).
  • يفترض أن بقية أجزاء النظام تتبع معاييرها: الخادم مثلاً يُقيَّم بمعيار OWASP ASVS.
  • يفترض أن المهاجم يستطيع الحصول على حزمة التطبيق وتحليلها، وتشغيله على جهاز مخترق، ومهاجمة الشبكة (MITM).
  • لا يضمن أماناً مطلقاً؛ هو خط أساس تُضاف إليه حمايات حسب مخاطر كل تطبيق.

على أي تطبيقات ينطبق؟

المعيار لا يهتم بطريقة البناء: التطبيقات الأصلية (Java/Kotlin وSwift/Objective-C)، ومتعددة المنصات (Flutter وReact Native وXamarin)، والهجينة (Cordova وIonic)، وكذلك التطبيقات المثبّتة مسبقاً من المصنع (Preloads) التي قد تملك صلاحيات أعلى، ومكتبات SDK التي يعتمد عليها التطبيق؛ فمتوسط التطبيق يستخدم نحو 30 SDK، ونحو 90% من كوده يأتي من أطراف ثالثة.

التقييم والشهادات

  • OWASP لا تمنح شهادات ولا تعتمد أي جهة أو تطبيق. أي "شهادة MASVS" ليست صادرة رسمياً عن OWASP، فانتبه لمدى ثقتك بها.
  • الطريقة الموصى بها هي مراجعة "الكتاب المفتوح" (open book): يحصل المختبِر على وصول للمعماريين والمطوّرين والتوثيق والكود، وعلى حساب لكل دور في التطبيق.
  • MASVS يغطي جهة العميل (التطبيق) فقط. الخدمات البعيدة تُختبر بدليل OWASP WSTG، وأجهزة إنترنت الأشياء بدليل OWASP ISTG.
  • الأدوات الآلية مفيدة للسرعة، لكنها لا تكفي وحدها؛ كل تطبيق مختلف ويجب فهم منطقه ومعماريته.
  • التقرير الجيد يذكر النطاق، والاختبارات الناجحة والفاشلة، وكيف تُصلح المشاكل، مع أدلة كافية (لقطات، سكربتات، سجلات البروكسي).

استخدامات أخرى للمعيار

مرجع لمعماريي الأمن لسد ثغرات إطارات مثل SABSA وTOGAF في جانب الموبايل، وبديل لقوائم الفحص الجاهزة، وأساس لمنهجيات الاختبار، ومصدر لاختبارات وحدة وتكامل آلية داخل دورة التطوير، ومنهج لتدريب المطوّرين على البناء الآمن بدلاً من الاكتفاء بدروس الاختراق.

The MASVS standard

MASVS is the specification sheet for mobile app security: a short, clear list of controls that any app can be measured against, however it was built.

What is MASVS?

The Mobile Application Security Verification Standard. It is used in three ways: as a metric to measure an app's security, as guidance developers follow while building, and as a baseline required when procuring an app or contracting its development. The current version is v2.1.0.

The security model: 8 groups

The standard splits the mobile attack surface into eight groups. Each one has an official color, used on the original site and in this guide:

GroupWhat it covers
STORAGESecure storage of sensitive data on the device (data at rest).
CRYPTOCryptography used to protect sensitive data, and its key management.
AUTHAuthentication (who are you?) and authorization (what are you allowed to do?).
NETWORKSecure communication with remote endpoints (data in transit).
PLATFORMSecure interaction with the OS, other apps and the user interface.
CODECode quality, input handling, and keeping the app up to date.
RESILIENCEResilience against reverse engineering and tampering.
PRIVACYProtecting user privacy.

How to read the IDs

A group is written MASVS-XXXXX and a control MASVS-XXXXX-Y. For example, MASVS-NETWORK-2 is the second control in the network group: identity pinning. The number is only the position inside the group, not a difficulty or importance level.

CODE-4The app validates and sanitizes all untrusted inputs.
RESILIENCE-1The app validates the integrity of the platform.
RESILIENCE-2The app implements anti-tampering mechanisms.
RESILIENCE-3The app implements anti-static analysis mechanisms.
RESILIENCE-4The app implements anti-dynamic analysis techniques.
PRIVACY-1The app minimizes access to sensitive data and resources.
PRIVACY-2The app prevents identification of the user.
PRIVACY-3The app is transparent about data collection and usage.
PRIVACY-4The app offers user control over their data.

Each group and its controls are explained in detail in Part 4 of the course.

The standard's assumptions

  • MASVS does not replace secure development practices (a secure SDLC) such as threat modeling and security reviews; it complements them for the mobile part. It points to frameworks such as OWASP SAMM and NIST SP 800-218 (SSDF).
  • It assumes the rest of the system follows its own standards: the backend, for example, is assessed with OWASP ASVS.
  • It assumes an attacker can obtain and analyze the app package, run it on a compromised device, and attack the network (MITM).
  • It does not guarantee absolute security. It is a baseline, and you add protections on top of it based on each app's risks.

Which apps does it apply to?

The standard does not care how an app is built: native apps (Java/Kotlin and Swift/Objective-C), cross-platform apps (Flutter, React Native, Xamarin), hybrid apps (Cordova, Ionic), as well as preloaded apps installed at the factory, which may have elevated privileges, and the SDKs an app depends on. The average app uses about 30 SDKs, and around 90% of its code comes from third parties.

Assessment and certification

  • OWASP does not certify any vendor, verifier or app. Any "MASVS certification" is not officially issued by OWASP, so be careful how much trust you place in it.
  • The recommended approach is an "open book" review: the tester gets access to the architects and developers, the documentation, the source code, and an account for every user role.
  • MASVS covers the client side (the app) only. Remote services are tested with the OWASP WSTG, and IoT devices with the OWASP ISTG.
  • Automated tools help with speed, but they are never enough on their own. Every app is different, and its logic and architecture must be understood.
  • A good report states the scope, the passed and failed tests, how to fix the failures, and enough evidence (screenshots, scripts, proxy logs).

Other uses of the standard

A reference for security architects, filling the mobile gaps in frameworks such as SABSA and TOGAF; a replacement for off-the-shelf secure coding checklists; a basis for testing methodologies; a source for automated unit and integration tests inside the development lifecycle; and a curriculum for training developers to build securely, instead of only teaching them how to hack.

أمثلة لتركيب الملفات

L1 + P

أخبار، تقويم

L1 + P + R

تطبيق طقس بإعلانات

L2 + P

مراسلة، صحة، رياضة

بيانات متوسطة أو عالية الخطورة (الموقع، الدفع، الصحة، التوكنات) ووظائف حساسة مثل رفع سجل طبي.

L2 + P + R

بنك، تأمين، ألعاب

بيانات عالية الخطورة، ووظائف حساسة (تحويل أموال، شراء داخل التطبيق)، وأصول تجارية.

كيف تختار الملف المناسب؟

  • الهدف ليس تطبيق كل الملفات وكل الاختبارات. ابدأ بنموذج التهديدات (Threat Model) ووظائف التطبيق وحساسية بياناته.
  • لا تطبّق اختبارات على ميزة غير موجودة: تطبيق بلا بصمة لا يحتاج اختبارات البصمة.
  • يمكن خلط المستويات داخل التطبيق نفسه: بيانات عالية الخطورة لا تغادر الجهاز قد تحتاج L2 في التخزين، بينما يكفي L1 في الشبكة.
  • أشرك كل أصحاب العلاقة في القرار، ووثّق أي استثناء ومبرّره.
  • أعلى درجة ضمان تأتي من ملف مخصص مبني على نموذج تهديد مفصّل؛ الملفات الافتراضية نقطة بداية جيدة للفرق ذات الوقت المحدود.

MAS testing profiles

Not every app needs the same level of protection. A weather app is not a banking app, and the profiles help you pick the right depth.

From "levels" to "profiles"

In MASVS v1, the levels L1, L2 and R were part of the standard itself. In v2 they moved out of the standard and became testing profiles, linked directly to the tests and weaknesses. That is why every weakness and every test has a profiles field, such as [L1, L2] or [R]. The idea: controls stay stable and general, while how strictly you test them depends on the app's context.

The four core profiles

They fall into two groups: security profiles (L1, L2 and R) that deal with attackers, and a privacy profile (P) that deals with protecting personal data.

ProfileWho is the adversary?Recommended for
MAS-L1
Essential security		Other apps installed on the device. The OS is trusted (the device is not rooted or jailbroken), and the user is not an adversary.Every app as a baseline, and apps with low-risk data and no sensitive functionality.
MAS-L2

MAS-R
Resilience		The device user may be the attacker: a reverse engineer or a cheater in a game.Apps that protect business assets and logic: games, DRM, license checks.
MAS-P

Keep in mind

MAS-R augments L1 and L2; it does not replace them. Missing R protections is not a vulnerability by itself, and those protections cannot guarantee 100% success, because the reverse engineer owns the device and will succeed given enough time and resources.

Examples of what calls for L2: health apps subject to laws such as HIPAA in the US or the European Health Data Space (EHDS), and finance apps that must comply with standards such as PCI DSS.

A specialized profile: MAS-EUDIW

Besides the four core profiles, there is a profile for the EU Digital Identity Wallet. It was developed with the Dutch digital identity program and partners from Belgium and Finland, and it turns regulatory requirements (such as eIDAS) into testable requirements mapped to MASWE weaknesses. It reuses most controls from the four core profiles and adds identity-specific assets such as Wallet Instance Attestations (WIA) and Person Identification Data (PID). It is recommended for high-assurance apps that must resist attackers with high attack potential.

Example profile combinations

L1 + P

News, calendar

No business assets, low-risk data (name, email), no sensitive functionality.

L1 + P + R

Ad-supported weather app

Low-risk data, but there is a business asset: intellectual property and ad revenue.

L2 + P

Messenger, health, sport

Moderate or high-risk data (location, payment, health, tokens) and sensitive functionality such as uploading a medical record.

L2 + P + R

Banking, insurance, games

High-risk data, sensitive functionality (money transfers, in-app purchases) and business assets.

How to choose the right profile

  • The goal is not to comply with every profile and every test. Start from the threat model, the app's functionality and the sensitivity of its data.
  • Don't apply tests to features that don't exist: an app without biometrics needs no biometric tests.
  • You can mix levels within the same app: high-risk data that never leaves the device may need L2 for storage, while L1 is enough for the network.
  • Involve all stakeholders in the decision, and document every deviation and its justification.
  • The highest assurance comes from a custom profile based on a detailed threat model. The default profiles are a good starting point for teams with limited time.

The trade-offs

Security vs cost

Add controls where the potential impact outweighs the cost of building and maintaining them. A news app rarely needs more than L1.

Security vs usability

Complex passwords and frequent re-verification frustrate users. Resolve these conflicts at design time.

Security vs privacy

Using SMS for two-factor authentication raises security, but it exposes the phone number, which is personal data.

Privacy vs value

Personalized recommendations and location-based discounts are useful, but they can expose users to tracking. Be transparent.

نقاط الضعف MASWE

إذا كان MASVS يقول "احمِ البيانات"، فإن MASWE يقول بالضبط: هذه هي الأخطاء التي تجعل البيانات غير محمية.

ما هو MASWE؟

Mobile Application Security Weakness Enumeration: قائمة بنقاط الضعف الأمنية والخصوصية الشائعة في تطبيقات الموبايل. هو الجسر بين ضوابط MASVS العامة واختبارات MASTG المحددة. استوحى فكرته من قائمة CWE العالمية، وهو مكمّل لها ومتخصص في الموبايل. الإصدار المستقر الأول v1.0.0 صدر في أغسطس 2026 ويضم 78 نقطة ضعف.

تشبيه بسيط

تخيّل مطعماً. المعيار يقول: "يجب أن يكون الطعام آمناً". نقطة الضعف: "حفظ اللحم خارج الثلاجة". الاختبار: "قِس حرارة الثلاجة بميزان حرارة وسجّل الرقم". وأفضل الممارسة: "اضبط الثلاجة على 4 درجات أو أقل".

لماذا نحتاج هذه الطبقة؟

عبارة "فشل التطبيق في MASVS-STORAGE-2" لا تخبر المطوّر بما يجب إصلاحه. أما "فشل في MASWE-0005 بسبب السجلات المفصّلة في نسخة الإنتاج" فهي نتيجة محددة يمكن العمل عليها مباشرة. ولأن المعايير أصبحت تُذكر في الشهادات واللوائح، فالدقة هنا مهمة.

تشريح صفحة نقطة الضعف

كل نقطة ضعف تبدأ ببيانات وصفية، أهمها:

  • requirement: جملة المتطلب الإيجابية، وهي عكس نقطة الضعف، وتصلح للسياسات والعقود. مثال: "The app excludes sensitive data from application logs".
  • platform وprofiles: المنصات والملفات التي تنطبق عليها.
  • mappings: الربط مع ضوابط MASVS v1 وv2، وأرقام CWE، ومخاطر Android الرسمية.

ثم أربعة أقسام بالترتيب نفسه دائماً:

  1. Overview (نظرة عامة): تبدأ بجملة تعريف بصيغة "This weakness occurs when…"، ثم شرح قصير بلا كود ولا خطوات اختبار.
  2. Modes of Introduction (طرق الظهور): الأسباب التي يُدخلها المطوّر فقط، وكلها قابلة للاختبار.
  3. Impact (الأثر): العواقب فقط، بمفردات موحّدة.
  4. Mitigations (المعالجات): تعليمات عملية بصيغة الأمر موجّهة للمطوّر.

مثال كامل مختصر: MASWE-0001

MASWE-0001

Sensitive Data Stored Unencrypted in Private Storage

بيانات حساسة مخزّنة دون تشفير في التخزين الخاص بالتطبيق

المتطلب: التطبيق يشفّر البيانات الحساسة المخزّنة في تخزينه الخاص.

طرق الظهور: كتابة البيانات دون تشفير، أو تشفيرها بمفتاح مضمّن في الكود، أو حفظ المفتاح بجانب البيانات، أو تشفير ضعيف، أو صلاحيات ملفات متساهلة (مثل MODE_WORLD_READABLE على Android أو kSecAttrAccessibleAlways على iOS)، أو إبقاء البيانات بعد انتهاء الحاجة إليها.

الأثر: كشف بيانات حساسة، وتجاوز المصادقة إذا سُرقت كلمات مرور أو توكنات.

المعالجات: قلّل التخزين المحلي، قيّد الوصول للملفات، احذف البيانات عند انتهاء الحاجة، احفظ المفاتيح في مخزن المفاتيح الخاص بالنظام، وشفّر البيانات المخزّنة.

MASWE weaknesses

If MASVS says "protect the data", MASWE says exactly which mistakes leave the data unprotected.

What is MASWE?

The Mobile Application Security Weakness Enumeration: a list of common security and privacy weaknesses in mobile apps. It is the bridge between the general MASVS controls and the specific MASTG tests. It is inspired by the global CWE list, complements it, and focuses on mobile. The first stable release, v1.0.0, came out in August 2026 with 78 weaknesses.

A simple analogy

Think of a restaurant. The standard says: "food must be safe". The weakness: "meat kept outside the fridge". The test: "measure the fridge temperature with a thermometer and record the number". And the best practice: "set the fridge to 4 degrees or lower".

Why do we need this layer?

"The app failed MASVS-STORAGE-2" doesn't tell a developer what to fix. "The app failed MASWE-0005 because of verbose logging in the production build" is a precise finding that can be acted on right away. And because these standards are now cited in certifications and regulations, that precision matters.

Anatomy of a weakness page

Each weakness starts with metadata. The most important fields:

  • requirement: the positive requirement statement, the inverse of the weakness, ready for policies and contracts. Example: "The app excludes sensitive data from application logs."
  • platform and profiles: the platforms and profiles it applies to.
  • mappings: links to MASVS v1 and v2 controls, CWE numbers and the official Android risks.

Then four sections, always in the same order:

  1. Overview: opens with a one-sentence definition in the form "This weakness occurs when…", followed by a short explanation with no code and no test steps.
  2. Modes of Introduction: only the causes a developer introduces, and all of them are testable.
  3. Impact: only the consequences, using a shared vocabulary.
  4. Mitigations: actionable instructions, written as commands to the developer.

A complete short example: MASWE-0001

MASWE-0001

Sensitive Data Stored Unencrypted in Private Storage

Requirement: the app encrypts sensitive data stored in private storage.

Modes of introduction: writing data unencrypted, encrypting it with a key hardcoded in the app, storing the key next to the data, weak encryption, loose file permissions (such as MODE_WORLD_READABLE on Android or kSecAttrAccessibleAlways on iOS), or keeping data after it is no longer needed.

Impact: compromise of sensitive data, and authentication bypass if passwords or tokens are stolen.

Mitigations: minimize local storage, restrict file access, remove data when it is no longer needed, keep keys in the platform keystore, and encrypt data at rest.

The golden rule linking MASWE to MASTG

Every mode of introduction should have a matching MASTG-TEST, usually one per platform. And every mitigation should have a matching MASTG-BEST best practice. This rule also shows what is still missing: at the v1.0.0 release, 36 of the 78 weaknesses had no test yet.

What changed since the beta?

  • From 119 to 78: duplicate entries were merged. For example, MASWE-0050 (Unsafe Handling of Untrusted Data) absorbs nine earlier entries, including SQL injection and insecure deserialization.
  • Stable IDs: the renumbering happened once, before v1.0.0. From now on no ID will move or be reused.
  • A shared impact vocabulary of ten labels, including: compromise of sensitive data, authentication or authorization bypass, bypass of protection mechanisms, execution of unauthorized code, financial loss, violation of user privacy, loss of user trust, and legal and regulatory non-compliance.
  • Deeper mappings: all 24 MASVS controls are covered, all 44 risks on Android's official risks page are mapped, and 93 distinct CWEs are referenced.

الاختبار في v2 فحص ذرّي: يتحقق من شيء واحد، وله بنية ثابتة. يبدأ ببيانات وصفية: المنصة، ونوع الاختبار (static أو dynamic أو code أو manual أو hooks)، ونقطة الضعف المرتبطة، والملفات، ومقالات المعرفة، وأفضل الممارسات، وواجهات البرمجة المعنية. ثم أربعة أقسام:

  4. Evaluation: كيف تقرر النجاح أو الفشل.

The MASTG testing guide

The MASTG is the hands-on part of the project: how to test a real app on Android and iOS step by step, and how to tell whether it passed or failed.

What a v2 test looks like

A v2 test is atomic: it checks one thing and has a fixed structure. It starts with metadata: the platform, the test type (static, dynamic, code, manual or hooks), the linked weakness, the profiles, the knowledge articles, the best practices and the APIs involved. Then four sections:

  1. Overview: what the test checks and why.
  2. Steps: what you do, using existing MASTG-TECH techniques.
  3. Observation: the raw output of your steps, before any interpretation.
  4. Evaluation: how you decide pass or fail.

A real example: MASTG-TEST-0326

MASTG-TEST-0326 · Android

References to APIs Allowing Fallback to Non-Biometric Authentication

Overview: does the app configure BiometricPrompt to allow falling back to the device credential (DEVICE_CREDENTIAL) for sensitive operations?

Steps: reverse engineer the app (MASTG-TECH-0013), then search for the relevant APIs (MASTG-TECH-0014).

Observation: a list of the places where those APIs are used.

Evaluation: the test fails if DEVICE_CREDENTIAL is allowed for a sensitive resource. The fix is in MASTG-BEST-0031.

Following the same logic, the old MASTG-TEST-0001 "Testing Local Storage for Sensitive Data" (152 lines) was split into independent tests such as MASTG-TEST-0207 (Runtime Storage of Unencrypted Data in the App Sandbox) and MASTG-TEST-0200 (Files Written to External Storage). Each one can be referenced, automated and failed on its own.

Demos

Every MASTG-DEMO consists of a sample app containing the weakness (Kotlin, Swift or Objective-C), a ready-to-run script (a static analysis command or a Frida script), an output.txt file with the expected output, and an evaluation section explaining what counts as a failure. All demos are built and tested automatically on every update, and they run inside the MAS Test Apps skeletons, so you can run them on your own device and check the results yourself.

أنواع تطبيقات الموبايل

قبل أن تفحص تطبيقاً اعرف كيف بُني، لأن طريقة البناء تغيّر مكان الثغرات والأدوات التي تحتاجها للبحث عنها.

يقسّم الفصل 0x04a من MASTG التطبيقات إلى خمسة أنواع:

1. التطبيقات الأصلية (Native)

تُكتب بلغة المنصة الرسمية: Java أو Kotlin على Android، وSwift أو Objective-C على iOS. تصل إلى كل واجهات النظام مباشرة، وأداؤها أعلى، وتستفيد من ميزات الأمان الخاصة بالمنصة مثل Keystore وKeychain. يمكن أن تحتوي أيضاً على كود أصلي بلغة C/C++ (مكتبات .so على Android).

2. الأطر متعددة المنصات (Cross-platform)

كود واحد يعمل على أكثر من نظام: Flutter (لغة Dart)، وReact Native (JavaScript)، وXamarin / .NET MAUI (C#). قد تحمل ثغرات خاصة بالإطار نفسه، وتحتاج أدوات تحليل خاصة؛ مثلاً blutter وreFlutter لتطبيقات Flutter، وhermes-dec لتطبيقات React Native المترجمة إلى Hermes bytecode.

3. تطبيقات الويب (Web Apps)

مواقع مصممة للهاتف تعمل داخل المتصفح. لا تُثبَّت من المتجر، ووصولها لميزات الجهاز محدود بما يسمح به المتصفح وصندوقه الأمني.

4. التطبيقات الهجينة (Hybrid)

تطبيق أصلي يعمل كـ "غلاف" يعرض واجهة ويب داخل WebView، ويتواصل مع ميزات الجهاز عبر جسر (bridge) برمجي. أمثلة: Cordova/PhoneGap وIonic وFramework7 وOnsen UI. الخطر الأبرز: ثغرة XSS في الصفحة قد تتحول إلى تنفيذ أوامر على الجهاز إذا كان الجسر يكشف وظائف حساسة.

5. تطبيقات الويب التقدمية (PWA)

مواقع يمكن "تثبيتها" من المتصفح على الشاشة الرئيسية، وتعمل دون اتصال بفضل Service Workers، وتستخدم بعض ميزات الجهاز مثل الإشعارات.

Types of mobile apps

Before you test an app, find out how it was built. The way it is built changes where the vulnerabilities hide and which tools you need to find them.

Chapter 0x04a of the MASTG divides apps into five types:

1. Native apps

Written in the platform's official language: Java or Kotlin on Android, and Swift or Objective-C on iOS. They reach every system API directly, perform best, and benefit from platform security features such as the Keystore and the Keychain. They can also contain native C/C++ code (.so libraries on Android).

2. Cross-platform frameworks

One codebase that runs on more than one OS: Flutter (Dart), React Native (JavaScript) and Xamarin / .NET MAUI (C#). They can carry framework-specific vulnerabilities and need dedicated analysis tools; for example blutter and reFlutter for Flutter apps, and hermes-dec for React Native apps compiled to Hermes bytecode.

3. Web apps

Websites designed for phones that run inside the browser. They aren't installed from a store, and their access to device features is limited to what the browser and its sandbox allow.

4. Hybrid apps

A native app that acts as a "shell" showing a web interface inside a WebView, and talks to device features through a code bridge. Examples: Cordova/PhoneGap, Ionic, Framework7 and Onsen UI. The biggest risk: an XSS flaw in the page can turn into command execution on the device if the bridge exposes sensitive functions.

5. Progressive web apps (PWA)

Websites you can "install" from the browser onto the home screen. They work offline thanks to Service Workers and use some device features such as notifications.

منهجية اختبار الأمان

الاختبار الأمني ليس "تشغيل أداة وطباعة النتائج". هو عملية منظّمة لها أنواع ومراحل، ويحتاج فهماً لسياق التطبيق.

حسب المعلومات المتاحة للمختبِر

Black-box

الصندوق الأسود

بلا أي معلومات عن التطبيق. يتصرف المختبِر كمهاجم حقيقي يعتمد على ما يمكن اكتشافه علناً.

White-box

الصندوق الأبيض

معرفة كاملة: الكود والتوثيق والمخططات. أسرع وأعمق، ويسمح بحالات اختبار أدق.

Gray-box

الصندوق الرمادي

بين الاثنين؛ يُعطى المختبِر بعض المعلومات (عادةً بيانات الدخول فقط). الأكثر شيوعاً في صناعة الأمن.

التحليل الساكن والديناميكي

التحليل الساكن (Static Analysis): فحص الكود أو حزمة التطبيق دون تشغيلها، يدوياً (مراجعة الكود سطراً سطراً) أو آلياً بأدوات SAST. جيد لاكتشاف الأسرار المضمّنة، والواجهات الخطرة، وإعدادات الملفات مثل AndroidManifest.xml.

التحليل الديناميكي (Dynamic Analysis): فحص التطبيق أثناء تشغيله: مراقبة الملفات التي يكتبها، والطلبات التي يرسلها، والذاكرة، وتعديل الدوال وقت التشغيل (hooking). يكشف ما لا يظهر في الكود، مثل البيانات الحقيقية المخزّنة.

الأفضل دائماً الجمع بين الاثنين؛ واختبارات MASTG v2 تحدد نوعها في حقل type.

تجنّب الإيجابيات الكاذبة (False Positives)

الأدوات الآلية لا تفهم السياق، فتبلّغ أحياناً عن مشاكل غير مؤثرة فعلياً. أمثلة من MASTG:

  • CSRF: أدوات فحص الخادم المصممة للمتصفحات تبلّغ عنه، لكنه غالباً لا يؤثر في تطبيق الموبايل، لأن أي رابط خبيث يضغطه المستخدم يفتح في المتصفح الافتراضي الذي له مخزن كوكيز منفصل.
  • الأرقام العشوائية: مولّد ضعيف لترتيب عناصر لعبة ليس مشكلة أمنية، لكنه مشكلة خطيرة إذا استُخدم لتوليد مفاتيح أو توكنات.
  • الترميز ليس تشفيراً: Base64 يُعكس بسهولة، فلا يحمي أي بيانات.
  • توكن API: تخزينه نصاً واضحاً خطر إذا كان لواجهة حساسة أو تسمح بالكتابة، وقد لا يكون خطراً لواجهة عامة للقراءة فقط.
القاعدة

فكّر في سيناريو الاستغلال الحقيقي عند تقييم الخطر، ولا تثق بمخرجات أداة الفحص بشكل أعمى.

حالات البيانات الحساسة الثلاث

  • مخزّنة (At rest): في ملف أو قاعدة بيانات.
  • قيد الاستخدام (In use): محمّلة في ذاكرة التطبيق.
  • منقولة (In transit): بين التطبيق والخادم، أو بين العمليات على الجهاز نفسه (IPC).

مراحل اختبار الاختراق (Penetration Testing)

  1. التحضير: تحديد النطاق، والضوابط المطبّقة، وأهداف المؤسسة، وتصنيف البيانات الحساسة، والحصول على تصريح مكتوب. مهاجمة نظام دون تصريح مخالفة للقانون في كثير من الدول.
  2. جمع المعلومات: فهم البيئة (أهداف العمل، قطاع التطبيق) والمعمارية: كيف يتعامل التطبيق مع البيانات، وإصدارات النظام المدعومة، والشبكة، والخدمات البعيدة.
  3. رسم خريطة التطبيق: نقاط الدخول، والبيانات، والثغرات المحتملة مرتّبة حسب خطورتها، وكتابة حالات الاختبار.
  4. الاستغلال: محاولة استغلال الثغرات للتأكد أنها حقيقية. يُقيَّم كل منها بخمسة عوامل: حجم الضرر، وسهولة تكرار الهجوم، وسهولة تنفيذه، وعدد المستخدمين المتأثرين، وسهولة اكتشاف الثغرة.
  5. التقرير: وصف الثغرات وطريقة استغلالها وتصنيفها وخطرها والبيانات التي أمكن الوصول إليها.

الأمان داخل دورة حياة التطوير (SDLC)

كلما اكتُشفت المشكلة مبكراً كان إصلاحها أرخص. الأنشطة الأمنية الأساسية: تحديد المتطلبات الأمنية وحالات الإساءة (abuse cases) مع المتطلبات الوظيفية، ثم نمذجة التهديدات أثناء التصميم، ثم التطوير الآمن بمراجعة الكود وأدوات SAST واختبارات الوحدة الأمنية، ثم اختبار الاختراق قبل الإطلاق، ثم المتابعة بعد النشر.

في نموذج Waterfall تأتي الاختبارات في مراحل محددة متتالية. أما في Agile وDevSecOps فتُؤتمت الأنشطة الأمنية قدر الإمكان وتجري طوال الدورة داخل خط التكامل والنشر المستمر (CI/CD)، وهو ما يُسمى "التحوّل إلى اليسار" (shift left).

Security testing methodology

Security testing is not "run a tool and print the results". It is an organized process with different types and phases, and it requires understanding the app's context.

By how much the tester knows

Black-box

Black-box testing

No information about the app. The tester behaves like a real attacker relying on what can be discovered publicly.

White-box

White-box testing

Full knowledge: source code, documentation and diagrams. Faster and deeper, and it allows more precise test cases.

Gray-box

Gray-box testing

In between: the tester gets some information (usually just credentials). The most common kind in the security industry.

Static and dynamic analysis

Static analysis: examining the code or the app package without running it, either manually (line-by-line code review) or with automated SAST tools. Good for finding hardcoded secrets, dangerous APIs and configuration files such as AndroidManifest.xml.

Dynamic analysis: examining the app while it runs: watching the files it writes, the requests it sends and its memory, and modifying functions at runtime (hooking). It reveals what the code alone doesn't show, such as the real data being stored.

The best results always come from combining both, and MASTG v2 tests state their type in the type field.

Avoiding false positives

Automated tools don't understand context, so they sometimes report issues that have no real impact. Examples from the MASTG:

  • CSRF: backend scanners built for browsers report it, but it usually doesn't affect a mobile app, because any malicious link the user taps opens in the default browser, which has a separate cookie store.
  • Random numbers: a weak generator for shuffling items in a game is not a security problem, but it is a serious one if it generates keys or tokens.
  • Encoding is not encryption: Base64 is easily reversed, so it protects nothing.
  • API tokens: storing one in plain text is risky for a sensitive or write-access API, and may not be for a public read-only API.
The rule

Think about the real exploit scenario when you assess the risk, and never trust your scanner's output blindly.

The three states of sensitive data

  • At rest: sitting in a file or a database.
  • In use: loaded into the app's memory.
  • In transit: moving between the app and the server, or between processes on the same device (IPC).

The phases of a penetration test

  1. Preparation: define the scope, the applicable controls, the organization's goals and the classification of sensitive data, and get written authorization. Attacking a system without permission is illegal in many countries.
  2. Intelligence gathering: understand the environment (business goals, the app's industry) and the architecture: how the app handles data, the OS versions it supports, the network and the remote services.
  3. Mapping the application: entry points, data, likely vulnerabilities ranked by severity, and the test cases to run.
  4. Exploitation: try to exploit the vulnerabilities to confirm they are real. Each one is rated on five factors: damage potential, reproducibility, exploitability, affected users and discoverability.
  5. Reporting: describe the vulnerabilities, how they were exploited, their classification and risk, and what data could be accessed.

Security inside the development lifecycle (SDLC)

The earlier a problem is found, the cheaper it is to fix. The core security activities: defining security requirements and abuse cases alongside the functional requirements, then threat modeling during design, then secure development with code reviews, SAST tools and security unit tests, then a penetration test before release, and follow-up after launch.

In a Waterfall model, testing happens in fixed, sequential phases. In Agile and DevSecOps, security activities are automated as much as possible and run throughout the lifecycle inside the CI/CD pipeline, which is what "shift left" means.

الهندسة العكسية والتلاعب

لتختبر تطبيقاً لا تملك كوده، عليك أن تفهمه من الداخل، وأحياناً أن تغيّر سلوكه لتتمكن من فحصه.

تعريفان أساسيان

الهندسة العكسية (Reverse Engineering): تحليل التطبيق المُترجَم لاستخراج معلومات عن كوده. هدفها الفهم.

التلاعب (Tampering): تعديل التطبيق (الملف المُترجَم أو العملية الجارية) أو بيئته لتغيير سلوكه. مثال: التطبيق يرفض العمل على جهازك المزوّد بصلاحيات root، فتعطّل هذا الفحص لتكمل اختباراتك.

لماذا يحتاجها المختبِر؟

  1. لتمكين اختبار الصندوق الأسود: حمايات مثل SSL Pinning والتشفير من طرف إلى طرف تمنعك من اعتراض الترافيك، وكشف الروت يمنعك من استخدام أدواتك. يجب أن تعرف كيف تعطّلها.
  2. لتقوية التحليل الساكن: فهم منطق التطبيق من الـ bytecode أو الكود الثنائي يكشف أخطاء مثل كلمات المرور المضمّنة.
  3. لتقييم الصمود: في تطبيقات MAS-R يلعب المختبِر دور المهندس العكسي ويحاول تجاوز الحمايات، ليقيس مدى فعاليتها.
الخبر الجيد والسيئ

يقول MASTG: "في النهاية، المهندس العكسي يفوز دائماً"، خاصة على الموبايل حيث لا يستطيع التطبيق زرع حمايات عميقة في النظام. Android المفتوح يسمح بتعديل النظام نفسه، وiOS يعطي تحكماً أقل للطرفين. لكن الحمايات المتقدمة (مكافحة التصحيح متعددة الخيوط، التشفير بالصندوق الأبيض، تعقيد مسار التحكم) قد تتطلب تحليلاً يدوياً طويلاً ومرهقاً.

Reverse engineering and tampering

To test an app whose source code you don't have, you must understand it from the inside, and sometimes change its behavior so you can test it at all.

Two key definitions

Reverse engineering: analyzing the compiled app to extract information about its source code. Its goal is comprehension.

Tampering: modifying the app (the compiled file or the running process) or its environment to change its behavior. Example: the app refuses to run on your rooted test device, so you disable that check to continue testing.

Why do testers need it?

  1. To enable black-box testing: defenses such as SSL pinning and end-to-end encryption stop you from intercepting traffic, and root detection stops you from using your tools. You need to know how to disable them.
  2. To strengthen static analysis: understanding the app's logic from its bytecode or binary reveals flaws such as hardcoded credentials.
  3. To assess resilience: for MAS-R apps, the tester plays the reverse engineer and tries to bypass the defenses to measure how effective they are.
The good news and the bad news

The MASTG says: "Ultimately, the reverse engineer always wins." That is especially true on mobile, where an app cannot plant deep, rootkit-like defenses in the system. Open Android lets reverse engineers modify the OS itself, while iOS gives both sides less control. The bad news: advanced protections (multi-threaded anti-debugging, white-box cryptography, control-flow transformations) can require long, exhausting manual analysis.

What does hooking look like?

A simplified teaching example with Frida that disables a root-check function in a practice app (the class and method names are made up):

Java.perform(function () {
  var RootCheck = Java.use("com.example.app.RootCheck");
  RootCheck.isDeviceRooted.implementation = function () {
    console.log("[*] isDeviceRooted() called, returning false");
    return false;   // the app now believes the device is clean
  };
});

This is exactly what the MASTG means when it says that any check performed only inside the app can be bypassed, which is why important security decisions belong on the server.

How do you get started?

The field can feel huge for beginners. The MASTG's advice: set up the basic tools, start with simple tasks and Crackmes, learn the assembly or bytecode, the OS and the obfuscation techniques you run into, and work your way up to harder targets. No single method always works; use whatever is most efficient and combine approaches.

The Android platform

To test an Android app you need to know how the system isolates apps from each other, what an APK is made of, and how to set up a test environment.

The system layers

From the bottom up: the Linux kernel, the hardware abstraction layer HAL, the native libraries and the ART runtime (Android Runtime), the Java API framework, and finally the apps. Apps are written in Java or Kotlin and compiled to Dalvik bytecode inside .dex files, which ART runs.

Defense in depth

System-wide security

Device encryption, the isolated Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) that the Keystore relies on (plus a separate StrongBox chip on some devices), and Verified Boot.

Software isolation

Separate Linux users and groups, SELinux policies for mandatory access control, and the permission system.

Network security

TLS by default, cleartext traffic blocked for modern apps, and support for DNS over TLS.

Anti-exploitation

ASLR, KASLR, PIE and DEP to randomize memory addresses and block data execution, and SECCOMP filters to restrict system calls.

There are three main kinds of permissions: normal ones granted automatically, dangerous (runtime) ones the app requests while running and the user approves, and signature ones granted only to apps signed with the same certificate.

The app sandbox

Each app gets its own Linux user (a unique UID), runs in a separate process, and has a private data directory at /data/data/<package> that other apps cannot read. All apps start from a parent process called Zygote, which preloads shared libraries and is then forked for every new app to make it launch faster.

Inside an APK

An APK file is a ZIP archive containing:

ItemContents
AndroidManifest.xmlThe app's identity: package name, permissions, components, minSdkVersion and targetSdkVersion, and sensitive options such as android:debuggable, android:allowBackup and android:exported.
classes.dexThe compiled code (Dalvik bytecode).
resources.arsc and res/Compiled resources: strings, layouts, images.
lib/Native .so libraries for each CPU architecture.
assets/Raw files the app reads (a common place for hidden secrets!).
META-INF/Signature files for the v1 scheme.

To publish on Google Play, apps are uploaded as an App Bundle (.aab), and the store generates the right APKs for each device from it.

The four components and Intents

  • Activities: the screens the user sees (which can be split into Fragments).
  • Services: background tasks without a user interface.
  • Broadcast Receivers: receive events from the system or other apps.
  • Content Providers: share data with other apps in a structured way.

Components talk to each other through Intents: explicit ones name the target component, and implicit ones describe an action and let the system choose who responds. Any exported component can be invoked by other apps, and that is one of the main things checked in the PLATFORM lesson.

Signing

Every APK must be signed with the developer's certificate, and an update is only accepted if it is signed with the same key.

  • Host computer: Android Studio and the Android SDK, plus the adb tool for talking to the device.
  • Test device: a real device with root access (usually via Magisk) or an Android Studio emulator.
  • Why root? To access the app's data directories, install the proxy certificate in the system store, and run frida-server.
  • Tools: Frida, objection, jadx, apktool, and an interception proxy such as Burp Suite, ZAP or mitmproxy.

The iOS platform

iOS is a more closed, tightly controlled system. That makes its default protection strong, and it means testing it needs special preparation.

Hardware security

Apple devices contain the Secure Enclave: a separate security processor that manages keys and protects Touch ID and Face ID data; the main OS cannot read its secrets directly. The chip also has a unique built-in key (the UID key) used for data encryption, which cannot be extracted.

Secure Boot

A chain of trust that starts from the read-only Boot ROM burned into the chip, then the iBoot bootloader, then the kernel. Each stage verifies Apple's signature on the next one before running it.

Code signing

Only code signed by Apple, or with a valid developer certificate, runs on iOS. Apps downloaded from the App Store are additionally encrypted with FairPlay, so they must be decrypted on a jailbroken device (for example with frida-ios-dump) before static analysis.

Data Protection

Every file is encrypted with its own key and tied to a protection class that decides when it can be decrypted:

ClassWhen the file can be read
CompleteOnly while the device is unlocked (the strongest).
CompleteUnlessOpenAn open file stays available after the device locks, and new files can be created while locked, but a closed file only opens while the device is unlocked.
CompleteUntilFirstUserAuthenticationAfter the first unlock since boot (the default class).
NoneProtected by the device key only; always available.

Sandbox and general protections

Every app runs inside its own container: a read-only bundle container, and a data container (Documents, Library and tmp). An app can only reach another app's data through deliberate mechanisms such as App Groups. Memory protections include ASLR (random addresses), XN (blocking execution of memory not meant for code), and ARC for automatic memory management.

Inside an IPA

An IPA file is a ZIP archive with a Payload/ folder containing App.app:

  • The executable in Mach-O format.
  • Info.plist: the app's settings, including permission prompt texts such as NSCameraUsageDescription, ATS settings and URL schemes.
  • embedded.mobileprovision and _CodeSignature/: the provisioning profile and signature.
  • Frameworks/: bundled libraries.

Special capabilities, the entitlements, are embedded in the signature: Keychain access groups, App Groups, and Associated Domains for universal links, for example.

Permissions and DeviceCheck

The app requests a permission when it needs it, with a justification text from Info.plist, and the user can revoke it later in Settings. App Attest (part of DeviceCheck) lets your server verify that a request comes from a genuine, unmodified copy of your app running on a real Apple device.

Setting up the test environment

  • Host computer: a Mac with Xcode and its command line tools is the easiest option, and many tools also run on Linux and Windows.
  • A jailbroken device: gives you root, file system access and the freedom to run Frida.
  • Alternatives: virtual devices such as Corellium, or the iOS Simulator (limited: it runs on the computer's architecture and can't run App Store apps, so it doesn't replace a real device).
  • Tools: Frida, objection, Keychain-Dumper, otool, class-dump and Burp Suite.

Data storage

Apps keep passwords, tokens and personal data on the device. Two questions drive this lesson: is what we store on purpose protected, and does anything leak by accident?

2 controls6 weaknessesMASTG: 0x05d, 0x06d

The controls

MASVS-STORAGE-1

The app securely stores sensitive data.

This covers intentional storage: data coming from the user, the backend or the system, whether stored in a location private to the app (internal storage) or in a public one that the user and other apps can reach (such as the Downloads folder). It must be protected wherever it is.

MASVS-STORAGE-2

The app prevents leakage of sensitive data.

This covers unintentional leaks as a side effect of using certain APIs or system features, such as logs and backups, in the cases where the developer can prevent them.

The explanation

What counts as "sensitive data"?

Personally identifiable information (PII), passwords, cryptographic keys, session tokens, API keys, and health and financial data. Exactly what is sensitive is defined during test preparation, based on the nature of the app.

The right approach, in short

  • Don't store what you don't need. Keep personal data on the server and show it when needed, and clear caches on logout.
  • Keys go only in the platform store: the Android Keystore or the iOS Keychain.
  • Encrypt sensitive files: on Android with EncryptedFile and EncryptedSharedPreferences (the Jetpack Security library is deprecated, but MASWE recommends using it until an official replacement is released), and on iOS with Data Protection.
  • Use the strictest Keychain accessibility you can, such as kSecAttrAccessibleWhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly, and avoid kSecAttrAccessibleAlways.
  • Avoid open file permissions such as the deprecated MODE_WORLD_READABLE, and share files through a properly configured FileProvider.

How is it tested?

Statically: look for calls such as getSharedPreferences, openFileOutput and the external storage APIs, and review the backup settings in the manifest. Dynamically: use the whole app, then inspect its directories (MASTG-TECH-0008), watch the logs (MASTG-TECH-0009), or trace file writes with Frida. Example tests: MASTG-TEST-0200 (Files Written to External Storage) and MASTG-TEST-0207 (Runtime Storage of Unencrypted Data in the App Sandbox).

Cryptography

A phone can be lost or stolen. Encryption is what makes the data worthless to anyone who gets it without permission, as long as it uses the right algorithm and a protected key.

2 controls11 weaknessesMASTG: 0x04g, 0x05e, 0x06e

The controls

MASVS-CRYPTO-1

The app employs current strong cryptography and uses it according to industry best practices.

The best practices here are defined by external standards such as NIST SP 800-175B and NIST SP 800-57.

MASVS-CRYPTO-2

The app performs key management according to industry best practices.

Even the strongest algorithm falls apart with poor key management. This control covers the key's entire lifecycle: generation, storage and protection.

The explanation

Simple concepts first

Symmetric encryption

One key to encrypt and decrypt, such as AES. Fast and suited to large amounts of data.

Asymmetric encryption

A public key you share and a secret private key, such as RSA and ECC curves. Used for signatures and key exchange.

Hashing

A one-way fingerprint that can't be reversed, such as SHA-256. For checking data integrity.

MACs and signatures

An HMAC proves a message is unchanged and came from someone holding the shared key. A digital signature proves the same with a private key.

Key derivation (KDF)

Turning a password into a strong key with a slow, salted function such as PBKDF2 or Argon2.

A secure random generator

An unpredictable source of randomness, the basis of every key and token.

What to avoid, and what to use instead

AvoidUse instead

Why is ECB bad?

It encrypts each block of data independently, so identical blocks produce identical ciphertext. The result: patterns in the original data stay visible despite the encryption.

Common configuration mistakes

  • Reusing a fixed IV or nonce with the same key.
  • Insufficient key length.
  • Using the same key pair for both signing and encryption.
  • Padding oracle attacks caused by modes such as CBC with weak padding and no integrity check.
  • Leaving keys in memory, or transporting them without protection.

Post-quantum

Future quantum computers could break today's public-key algorithms such as RSA and ECC. That is why, in 2024, NIST approved FIPS 203 (CRYSTALS-Kyber, also known as ML-KEM) for quantum-resistant key exchange. The current MASTG recommendations align with NIST beyond 2030, but they don't fully account for advances in quantum computing.

Authentication and authorization

Authentication asks: who are you? Authorization asks: what are you allowed to do? In apps that talk to a backend, the final decision must always be made on the server.

3 controls8 weaknessesMASTG: 0x04e, 0x05f, 0x06f

The controls

MASVS-AUTH-1

The app uses secure authentication and authorization protocols and follows the relevant best practices.

Enforcement happens on the server, but the app must use the protocols securely. The security of the server itself is assessed with OWASP ASVS.

MASVS-AUTH-2

The app performs local authentication securely according to the platform best practices.

Fingerprint, face and local PIN must be implemented correctly, especially in apps with no backend that rely entirely on local authentication.

MASVS-AUTH-3

The app secures sensitive operations with additional authentication.

For example, confirming a money transfer with a fingerprint or a code. It can be done with biometrics, a PIN, an MFA code generator, email or deep links, and all of them must be implemented securely.

The explanation

Rule number one: the server is the judge

Any check performed only inside the app can be bypassed, as you saw in the reverse engineering lesson. The server must verify the user's identity and permissions on every request, and never trust a decision that comes from the app.

Stateful and stateless sessions

Stateful

Stateful authentication

The server keeps the session and gives the app a long, random session ID. It must be unguessable, expire after inactivity, and be invalidated on logout.

Stateless · JWT

Stateless authentication

The server issues a signed token that carries the information. Always verify the signature and never accept alg: none, keep the lifetime short, and don't put sensitive data inside it, because it is only Base64-encoded, not encrypted.

OAuth 2.0

  • Use the Authorization Code flow with PKCE (Proof Key for Code Exchange), and make sure the server implements it too.
  • For signing in with external accounts (such as social media accounts), use an external user agent: the system browser or Custom Tabs. Avoid an embedded WebView here, because the host app can record whatever the user types.
  • In closed ecosystems, such as a banking app with its own server, an embedded user agent can be acceptable, since phishing between apps isn't a realistic scenario there.

Logout, passwords and two-factor authentication

  • Logout must end the session on the server, not just delete the token from the app.
  • Passwords: a sensible policy, protection against repeated guessing (rate limiting), and support for password managers through autofill.
  • SMS-OTP is weaker than the alternatives: it can be intercepted, or the number stolen through a SIM swap. Stronger options: TOTP apps, or transaction signing through push notifications with public and private keys.
  • Show the login activity history, and let users block unknown devices.

Local authentication: events vs cryptography

event-based

Event-based

The app asks the system "did the fingerprint succeed?" and waits for a yes or no. An attacker who owns the device can fake the answer by hooking. Weak.

crypto-based

Crypto-based

A successful scan unlocks a key inside the Keystore or Keychain, and that key is required to decrypt data or sign a request. There is no "answer" to fake. Strong.

هجوم الرجل في المنتصف (MITM)

كيف يتحقق التطبيق من الخادم؟

  • TrustManager مخصص يقبل أي شهادة دون فحص.
  • HostnameVerifier يُرجع true دائماً.
  • في WebView: استدعاء handler.proceed() داخل onReceivedSslError لتجاهل أخطاء الشهادة.
  • استثناءات واسعة تسمح بالاتصال غير المشفّر.

إعدادات TLS الموصى بها

TLS 1.2 أو 1.3 فقط، ومجموعات تشفير قوية (cipher suites) تدعم السرية الأمامية (Forward Secrecy). أما SSL 3.0 وTLS 1.0 و1.1 فقديمة وغير آمنة.

Network communication

Every request that leaves the app may cross a public Wi-Fi network or an attacker's device in the middle. The goal: an encrypted channel with a server whose identity is actually verified.

2 controls3 weaknessesMASTG: 0x04f, 0x05g, 0x06g

The controls

MASVS-NETWORK-1

The app secures all network traffic according to the current best practices.

Usually through TLS, which encrypts the data and authenticates the server. The risk is that developers can disable the platform's secure defaults, or bypass them entirely with low-level APIs or third-party libraries.

MASVS-NETWORK-2

The app performs identity pinning for all remote endpoints under the developer's control.

Instead of trusting every certificate authority (CA) on the device, the app trusts only specific CAs or keys. This is usually called certificate pinning or public key pinning.

The explanation

Man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks

The attacker places themselves between the app and the server, for example with a rogue Wi-Fi access point or by ARP spoofing on the local network, and reads and modifies the requests. If the connection isn't encrypted, or the app accepts any certificate, the attacker sees everything.

How does the app verify the server?

During a TLS connection the app checks that the certificate chain ends at a trusted authority in the trust store, that the domain name matches the certificate (the hostname), and that the certificate is valid and not expired. Common mistakes that break all of this:

  • A custom TrustManager that accepts any certificate without checking.
  • A HostnameVerifier that always returns true.
  • In a WebView: calling handler.proceed() inside onReceivedSslError to ignore certificate errors.
  • Broad exceptions that allow unencrypted connections.

Recommended TLS settings

TLS 1.2 or 1.3 only, with strong cipher suites that support forward secrecy. SSL 3.0, TLS 1.0 and TLS 1.1 are old and insecure.

Pinning wisely

Pinning protects against a compromised certificate authority or a certificate the attacker installed on the device. But it needs planning: a backup key, an expiration date, and a plan to update the app before the server certificate changes, otherwise the app is cut off from its server entirely. It applies only to servers the developer controls. Pinning the public key (SPKI) is more flexible than pinning the whole certificate.

How is it tested?

  1. Set up an interception proxy such as Burp Suite, ZAP or mitmproxy (MASTG-TECH-0011 on Android and MASTG-TECH-0063 on iOS) and install its certificate on the device.
  2. If the traffic shows up in clear without modifying the app, the app doesn't pin. If plain HTTP traffic shows up, the connection isn't encrypted at all.
  3. If the connection fails with the proxy certificate, pinning is in place. The tester can bypass it to continue (MASTG-TECH-0012 and MASTG-TECH-0064) with tools such as objection or Frida.
  4. For non-HTTP traffic use tools such as MITM Relay, and to check whether bad certificates are accepted you can use the badssl domains.

  • Intents: للتواصل الداخلي استخدم Intents صريحة. الضمنية قد يلتقطها تطبيق خبيث سجّل نفس الـ filter.
  • PendingIntent: استخدم FLAG_IMMUTABLE مع Intent صريح، حتى لا يعدّل تطبيق آخر وجهته أو محتواه.
  • Content Providers: قيّد الوصول بالأذونات، واستخدم استعلامات مُعَلْمَنة لمنع حقن SQL.
  • الروابط العميقة (Deep Links): تحقق من كل المدخلات القادمة عبرها. وروابط App Links مع autoVerify وملف assetlinks.json على نطاقك تمنع تطبيقات أخرى من خطفها.

IPC على iOS

  • Custom URL Schemes مثل myapp://: أي تطبيق آخر يستطيع تسجيل المخطط نفسه، لذا تحقق من المدخلات ومن التطبيق المصدر.
  • Universal Links: مرتبطة بنطاق يملكه المطوّر عبر ملف apple-app-site-association، فهي أكثر أماناً.
  • آليات أخرى تُفحص: إضافات التطبيق (App Extensions)، والحافظة (UIPasteboard)، وApp Groups، ومجموعات Keychain المشتركة، ومشاركة المحتوى عبر UIActivity، وApp Intents التي قد تكشف وظائف لـ Siri ووكلاء الذكاء الاصطناعي.

Platform interaction

An app doesn't live alone: it talks to other apps, opens links, shows web pages and displays data on screen. Each of those doors needs a lock.

3 controls12 weaknessesMASTG: 0x05h, 0x06h

The controls

MASVS-PLATFORM-1

The app uses IPC mechanisms securely.

Apps deliberately expose data or functionality through the platform's IPC mechanisms, and other installed apps and the user interact with them. All of that interaction must happen securely.

MASVS-PLATFORM-2

The app uses WebViews securely.

WebViews must be configured to prevent sensitive data leakage and the exposure of sensitive functionality, for example through JavaScript bridges to native code.

MASVS-PLATFORM-3

The app uses the user interface securely.

Passwords, card numbers and OTP codes in notifications appear on screen, and they must not leak through automatic screenshots, shoulder surfing or sharing the device with someone else.

The explanation

IPC on Android

  • Exported components: any Activity, Service, Receiver or Provider with android:exported="true" can be invoked by any app. Since Android 12 this value must be declared explicitly for every component with an intent-filter. Export only what is needed, and protect it with signature permissions.
  • Intents: use explicit intents for internal communication. Implicit ones can be caught by a malicious app that registered the same filter.
  • PendingIntent: use FLAG_IMMUTABLE with an explicit intent, so another app can't change its target or contents.
  • Content Providers: restrict access with permissions, and use parameterized queries to prevent SQL injection.
  • Deep links: validate every input that arrives through them. App Links with autoVerify and an assetlinks.json file on your domain stop other apps from hijacking them.

IPC on iOS

  • Custom URL schemes such as myapp://: any other app can register the same scheme, so validate the inputs and the source app.
  • Universal Links: tied to a domain the developer owns through an apple-app-site-association file, so they are safer.
  • Other mechanisms to check: app extensions, the pasteboard (UIPasteboard), App Groups, shared Keychain access groups, content sharing through UIActivity, and App Intents that may expose functionality to Siri and AI agents.

Why are bridges dangerous?

Any page loaded in the WebView, including one where an attacker injected XSS code, can call the exposed native functions. If those functions read files or send data, you have handed the attacker the keys to the app.

  • Screenshots and screen recording: on Android, use FLAG_SECURE for sensitive screens. On iOS, hide sensitive content before the system takes the app switcher snapshot when the app goes to the background.

أخطاء الذاكرة وحمايات المترجم

Code quality

Up-to-date, clean code stops many attacks before they start: a recent platform, enforced updates when needed, libraries without known vulnerabilities, and validation of every input.

4 controls10 weaknessesMASTG: 0x04h, 0x05i, 0x06i

The controls

MASVS-CODE-1

The app requires an up-to-date platform version.

Every new OS release brings security patches and new protections. Supporting very old versions leaves users exposed to well-known threats.

MASVS-CODE-2

The app has a mechanism for enforcing app updates.

Sometimes a critical vulnerability is found after the app is already in production. There must be a way to force users to update before they can keep using it.

MASVS-CODE-3

The app only uses software components without known vulnerabilities.

A full review of every third-party library is usually not feasible, but the minimum is to detect libraries with known vulnerabilities by scanning them.

MASVS-CODE-4

The app validates and sanitizes all untrusted inputs.

Data arriving through the UI, IPC, the network and files may have been modified by an untrusted party, and can lead to bypassed security checks or injection attacks.

The explanation

Platform version and updates

  • Android: minSdkVersion sets the oldest version the app runs on, and targetSdkVersion sets the security behavior it commits to. Target the latest version.
  • iOS: the Deployment Target in Xcode.
  • Enforcing updates: on Android with the Play In-App Updates API in "immediate" mode, and on iOS by checking the version number from the server and showing a mandatory screen. Most importantly, the server should also reject old versions.

Third-party libraries

Most of an app's code today comes from third parties. Scan dependencies with tools such as OWASP dependency-check and dependency-track, generate a software bill of materials (SBOM) in CycloneDX format with tools such as cdxgen, and follow the libraries' security updates.

Untrusted input

Its sources: the UI, IPC, the network, the file system and deep links. The related attacks:

  • SQL injection: in string-built queries and in Content Providers.
  • XML injection and XML external entities (XXE).
  • XSS inside a WebView, which can turn into command execution if a bridge is exposed.
  • Insecure deserialization of objects coming from outside.
  • Dynamic code loading from an untrusted source.
// Unsafe: user input concatenated into the query
db.rawQuery("SELECT * FROM users WHERE name = '$name'", null)

// Safe: a parameterized query, the value is passed separately
db.rawQuery("SELECT * FROM users WHERE name = ?", arrayOf(name))

Memory bugs and compiler protections

Native code (C/C++) can contain bugs such as buffer overflows and use-after-free. They are hard to find in a penetration test but easy to prevent with secure coding and compiler protections:

  • PIE (Position Independent Code): lets the code load at random addresses together with ASLR.
  • Stack canaries: a guard value that detects stack overwrites.
  • ARC on iOS for automatic memory management.

Other things the MASTG checks: removing debug symbols from the production build, handling exceptions without revealing internal details, and not leaving StrictMode or android:debuggable enabled in production.

Resilience against reverse engineering

An extra layer of defense that makes understanding and tampering with the app harder and more expensive. It doesn't replace the core controls, but it matters for protecting a company's assets and logic.

4 controls15 weaknessesMASTG: 0x04c, 0x05j, 0x06j

The controls

MASVS-RESILIENCE-1

The app validates the integrity of the platform.

Many controls (secure storage, biometrics, the sandbox) assume the OS is intact. Running on a modified OS can disable security features.

MASVS-RESILIENCE-2

The app implements anti-tampering mechanisms.

Without protection it is easy to run a modified copy (to cheat, or to unlock paid features) or to publish a backdoored copy in unofficial stores.

MASVS-RESILIENCE-3

The app implements anti-static analysis mechanisms.

Understanding an app is usually the first step before tampering with it, so the goal is to make figuring out how it works from its code as hard as possible.

MASVS-RESILIENCE-4

The app implements anti-dynamic analysis techniques.

Observing and manipulating an app at runtime makes it much easier to understand. The goal is to make that hard, and to block tools that modify code at runtime.

The explanation

Why do companies care?

To protect algorithms, trade secrets and customer data, and to avoid financial losses from cheating and piracy, legal trouble and reputational damage. But remember: missing these measures doesn't cause a vulnerability by itself. They are extra protection based on the threat model, and the app must meet the other MASVS controls first.

Defenses by control

ControlExample protections
RESILIENCE-1Root and jailbreak detection (su binaries, Magisk, apps such as Cydia and Sileo, writable system paths), detection of emulators, virtual devices and app virtualization environments, and detecting an iOS app running on macOS. Plus device attestation on the server: Google Play Integrity API and Key Attestation on Android, App Attest and DeviceCheck on iOS.
RESILIENCE-2Verifying the app's signature, integrity checks on files and resources, runtime code integrity checks, repackaging detection, and device binding.
RESILIENCE-3Obfuscation: renaming classes and methods with tools such as R8/ProGuard, dProtect or SwiftShield, string encryption, control-flow obfuscation, resource obfuscation, and removing debug symbols and debug code.
RESILIENCE-4Debugger detection (such as ptrace and Debug.isDebuggerConnected()), detection of tools such as Frida (the frida-server process, the default port 27042, its libraries in /proc/self/maps) and Xposed/LSPosed, and disabling android:debuggable.

RASP: runtime application self-protection

Runtime Application Self-Protection solutions gather these signals (root, debugger, Frida, tampering) and react to them, for example by ending the session or alerting the server. Libraries mentioned in the MASTG include RootBeer and IOSSecuritySuite.

Principles that make protections stronger

  • Multiple layers instead of a single check that one line of Frida can disable.
  • Continuous checks in different places and at different times, not once at startup.
  • Varied, delayed reactions instead of an instant "Root detected!" message that shows the attacker where the check is.
  • Send the signals to the server and let it decide, because the attacker can't modify what happens on the server.
Remember

These measures can't guarantee 100% effectiveness, because the reverse engineer has full control of the device. Their goal is to raise the cost and effort enough to deter most attackers. The Crackmes are perfect for practicing how to bypass them.

Privacy

Privacy doesn't only mean "don't leak data". It also means: don't collect more than you need, tell users clearly, and give them control over their data.

4 controls13 weaknessesMASTG: 0x04i

The controls

MASVS-PRIVACY-1

The app minimizes access to sensitive data and resources.

Request only what you truly need, with the user's informed consent. Share data with third parties only when necessary, stop SDKs from collecting anything before consent is confirmed, and keep track of the dependency chain the data passes through.

MASVS-PRIVACY-2

The app prevents identification of the user.

Through unlinkability techniques such as data abstraction, anonymization and pseudonymization. When fingerprint-like signals (device IDs, IP addresses, behavior patterns) are used for a specific purpose such as fraud detection, they are kept separate and not reused for analytics.

MASVS-PRIVACY-3

The app is transparent about data collection and usage.

Users have the right to know how their data is collected, stored and shared, including any behavior they wouldn't reasonably expect, such as background collection, while following the platform's guidelines on data declarations.

MASVS-PRIVACY-4

The app offers user control over their data.

Ways to manage, delete and modify data, change privacy settings and withdraw consent, and asking for consent again whenever the app needs more data than it originally declared.

The explanation

The scope of this group

MASVS-PRIVACY is a baseline for user privacy that focuses on the app itself, and on what can be checked from public information or through static and dynamic analysis. It doesn't replace comprehensive legal assessments such as the Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) required by the EU's GDPR; those are done by privacy experts, not only security experts.

Collecting and sharing are the same thing

In MASTG tests, "collecting" and "sharing" are treated the same way: whether the app sends data to a server or to another app on the device, the data has left the user's control. What happens to data on remote servers is outside the standard's scope, because it usually can't be checked.

Minimizing data (PRIVACY-1)

  • Ask for the narrowest permission: approximate location instead of precise, the photo picker instead of access to the whole gallery.
  • Request permissions at the moment they are needed, and explain why.
  • Watch the ad and analytics SDKs you embed; you are responsible for what they collect.

Preventing identification (PRIVACY-2)

  • Anonymization: removing any link to the person for good.
  • Pseudonymization: replacing the identity with an ID that only reveals the person when combined with additional information kept separately.
  • Avoid fixed hardware identifiers for tracking, and use the identifiers meant for that purpose, such as the Advertising ID on Android and the IDFA on iOS, which requires consent through the App Tracking Transparency framework.

A clear button to delete the account and its data, a way to edit the data, the option to withdraw consent at any time, privacy-friendly defaults, and asking for consent again whenever collection expands.

How is it tested?

Compare what the app declares in the store and its privacy policy with what it actually sends (intercept the traffic and watch the SDKs), check the permissions requested in AndroidManifest.xml and Info.plist, and verify that options to delete data and withdraw consent exist. Some checks can be automated, and some are manual by nature.

A suggested learning path

  1. Set up your environment: an emulator or a rooted device, with adb, jadx, Frida and an interception proxy.
  2. Solve Android UnCrackable L1 twice: once by reading the code in jadx, and once by hooking with Frida.
  3. Open a practice app such as AndroGoat or DIVA, and map every vulnerability you find to its MASWE ID and MASVS control.
  4. Run one of the MASTG demos on your device, and compare your output with its output.txt.
  5. Pick one MASTG test, follow its steps on a practice app, and write a short report with Observation and Evaluation sections.
